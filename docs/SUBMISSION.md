## Video script (target 4 minutes)

The brief requires five parts.

### 1. Problem statement (0:00–0:35)
Amani, a coffee farmer near Nyeri, at the farm gate. A buyer offers 105 shillings a kilo. She has a basic phone, patchy data and no way to check the offer. Read the one-sentence problem statement.

### 2. AI capabilities and why not SMS, a spreadsheet or search (0:35–1:20)
- **What the AI does:** it *listens* in the caller's language (Whisper speech recognition and language ID), *understands* a messy spoken offer (a small LLM pulls out price, unit, coffee form, grade and district), and *answers* naturally from approved data (Gemma 3 grounded in the co-op profile).
- **Why simpler tools fall short:** an SMS price list can't hear her quote, match its grade, unit and district, or tell her the gap. A spreadsheet needs literacy and a smartphone. Search needs data and doesn't know the co-op's records.
- **Guardrails, said out loud:** every number comes from a stored record, never the model. Out-of-date, thin or mismatched data means "not sure, ask the co-op". "Officer" or pressing 0 reaches a person. The farmer makes the decision.

### 3. Tool demo, the end-to-end user journey (1:20–3:00)
Screen recording of the web UI, with Wi-Fi visibly **off**:
1. English price check → `ANSWER`: 120 reference, 15 below, with date and source.
2. The same check **in Swahili** → Swahili answer.
3. `Parchment grade A in Kiambu` → `ABSTAIN`: record out of date.
4. `Do you sell tractors?` → "I don't have that information" plus an offer of a person.
5. Hindi business question → Hindi answer from the co-op profile.
6. `Can I talk to somebody?` → `REFER`.
7. Optional, Wi-Fi on: call the Twilio number from a basic phone and hear the same answers. Say plainly that the call path needs internet today and that a SIM gateway removes that.

Show the evidence panel (transcript, extracted fields, record ID) for at least one answer.

### 4. The challenge/gap: where it sits in her day, with tech stack (3:00–3:40)
- **When:** at the farm gate or before delivery day, the moment a buyer names a price.
- **What she does:** calls the co-op number (or uses the family smartphone), says the offer, hears the reference in under 30 seconds, then decides.
- **What happens next:** she sells, negotiates or waits. If the data was missing, a co-op officer calls back.
- **Stack:** faster-whisper small · rules + our fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B `coop-extract` (94.6% vs 81.1% for the 3B it replaced) · Gemma 3 4B · SQLite · Piper/ElevenLabs · Gradio · FastAPI + Twilio · Cloudflare tunnel. Everything except the optional voice and phone routing runs on one laptop CPU.

### 5. Your take: what localising AI development means to us (3:40–4:10)
_Write this in the team's own words._ Prompts to start from:
- Small models running where the farmer is, not a data centre she can't reach.
- Local languages first, and being honest about Gĩkũyũ, which today's models don't serve.
- Data that belongs to the co-op, and a tool that says "I don't know" rather than guessing about someone's income.
